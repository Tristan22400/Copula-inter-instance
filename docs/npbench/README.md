# EEG joint-likelihood benchmark (neural-process suites)

CopulaTabICL on the EEG tasks of two neural-process papers, which score the **joint** predictive
density of a set of target points:

- **Bruinsma et al., "Autoregressive Conditional Neural Processes", ICLR 2023** (arXiv 2303.14468;
  code `wesselb/neuralprocesses` 0.2.7). Exact protocol: the tasks are regenerated by the paper's
  own generator.
- **Hassan et al., "Efficient Autoregressive Inference for Transformer Probabilistic Models",
  ICLR 2026** (arXiv 2510.09477). Their EEG code is not released (`acerbilab/transformer-ar-buffer`
  is a placeholder; `conorhassan/AR-TabPFN` holds only the tabular model, trained on a synthetic
  SCM prior, with no EEG code or weights), so the tasks are rebuilt from the paper's text.

Methods, for each of three pretrained marginals (TabICLv2, TabPFN-3.5 Fast, Kumo small):

| Row | Joint predictive of the target set |
|---|---|
| `+ indep` | the marginal, independent across targets: $\sum_i \log f_i(y_i)$ |
| `+ copula` | the same marginal and its Gaussian copula: $\sum_i \log f_i(y_i) - \tfrac12(\log\det\Sigma + z^\top\Sigma^{-1}z - z^\top z)$, $z_i=\Phi^{-1}(F_i(y_i))$ |
| `+ AR` | the same marginal, chain rule with the true values fed back: $\sum_k \log f(y_{\pi_k}\mid \mathcal C, y_{\pi_{<k}})$ |

Every score is divided by the number of target scalars (nats per target value; higher is better),
as `nps.num_data` does. The copula's $\Sigma$ has a unit diagonal, so the copula row has exactly
the indep row's marginals; the report checks this on joint draws as well.

Copulas: `checkpoints/copula_nano/pretrained-marginal-<marginal>/step_0100000.pt`, each trained (100k
steps, P, N ∈ [10, 1000], rank 128, synthetic GP prior) on that marginal's **pretrained** PIT, and
only ever scored with that marginal.

## Commands

All heavy steps run on a Grid5000 node (never on the laptop). From the checkout root:

```bash
# 1. Generator venv (CPU torch + neuralprocesses 0.2.7; separate from the main venv), then the data.
bash scripts/npbench/install_np_venv.sh
bash scripts/npbench/fetch_eeg.sh "$REAL_DATA_DIR/npbench/eeg_raw"
.venv-npbench/bin/python -I scripts/npbench/export_eeg.py --out-dir "$REAL_DATA_DIR/npbench"

# 2. Score, one job per marginal (GPU; main venv via scripts/_env.sh).
python3 scripts/g5k_gpus.py submit --walltime 24 --name eeg-joint-tabicl -- \
    scripts/npbench/eeg_joint.sh marginals=[tabicl] out_dir=results/npbench/eeg ar.arcnp_batches=4 \
    ar.hassan_trials=32 timing.episodes=8 timing.samples=8
python3 scripts/g5k_gpus.py submit --walltime 24 --name eeg-joint-tabpfn -- \
    scripts/npbench/eeg_joint.sh marginals=[tabpfn35_fast] out_dir=results/npbench/eeg ar.arcnp_batches=4 \
    ar.hassan_trials=32 timing.episodes=8 timing.samples=8
python3 scripts/g5k_gpus.py submit --walltime 24 --name eeg-joint-kumo -- \
    scripts/npbench/eeg_joint.sh marginals=[kumo_small] out_dir=results/npbench/eeg ar.arcnp_batches=4 \
    ar.hassan_trials=32 timing.episodes=8 timing.samples=8
# (a job may be split by task family, e.g. tasks=[hassan_for] timing.enabled=false: finished units are skipped)

# 3. Tables (results/npbench/eeg/report.md).
python -m eval.runners.eeg_joint_eval command=report out_dir=results/npbench/eeg
```

`python -m eval.runners.eeg_joint_eval --cfg job` lists every key (task subset, AR subset sizes,
number of AR orders, timing settings).

## Protocols

### Bruinsma et al. (2023): `arcnp_interpolation`, `arcnp_reconstruction`

Regenerated with `nps.EEGGenerator(subset="eval")`, exactly as `experiment/data/eeg.py` and
`train.py::eval` do: the subject split with the fixed seed 99 (10 evaluation subjects), electrodes
FZ, F1–F6, generator seed 0, batches of 16 tasks sharing one context/target split, 2^12 tasks.
Raw microvolts, no standardisation.

- Interpolation: per electrode, U{50..256} random time points are targets; the rest is context.
- Reconstruction: one random electrode is cut at $t_0\sim U[0.5, 0.75]$ s (the later part is the
  target); the other six electrodes are fully observed.

Reported as mean ± 1.96 SE over the tasks (the paper's `with_err`).

### Hassan et al. (2026): `hassan_int`, `hassan_for`

Every evaluation trial (the 10 Bruinsma evaluation subjects), each electrode standardised, with
$N\in\{8,16,32,64,128,192\}$ context time points and $M\in\{16,64\}$ target time points (all 7
electrodes at each). Interpolation: $N+M$ distinct random time points; forecasting: the first $N$
time points, then the next $M$. Reported as mean (SEM) over trials × N.

## Deviations (and why)

1. **Scalar-output model.** The 7 electrodes are stacked as rows with features
   [time, one-hot electrode]; the published models have a 7-output head. The joint density is the
   same object (all target values of a task), but our AR chain runs over the $7M$ scalars, not the
   $M$ time points.
2. **Hassan et al. reconstruction** (their code is unreleased):
   - standardisation: per-electrode mean/std over the training subjects' trials (the statistics are
     not stated);
   - test trials: Bruinsma's 10 evaluation subjects (the paper says 10 test subjects and also "20");
   - normalisation: per target scalar ($7M$); the paper says "divided by $M$". If their numbers are
     per time point, they are 7× ours in scale — compare our rows with each other first;
   - interpolation draws context and targets uniformly among the 256 time points (the sampling of
     test inputs is not stated).
3. **AR rows on a subset.** Our AR chain re-encodes the context at every step (no KV cache), so it
   runs on 4 batches (64 tasks) of each Bruinsma task and 32 trials of each Hassan (N, M), **evenly
   spread** over the set (`eeg_tasks.spread`; both generators order episodes by subject, so leading
   episodes would be one subject). Hassan's AR rows average 128 target orders, ours
   `ar.orders_hassan` = 4 (log-mean-exp, as theirs); Bruinsma's use one random order, as ours. Each
   AR cell also prints indep and copula on the same episodes, and the paired table compares them.
4. **Context PIT:** K = 5 folds, as in the copulas' training data (eval_checkpoint's default is 10).
5. **Generation on CPU** with torch 2.5.1: neuralprocesses notes that its random stream depends on the
   device, so the tasks are statistically, not bitwise, those of the paper.
6. **Data source.** wbml's URL (`kdd.ics.uci.edu/.../eeg_full.tar`) returns 403; `fetch_eeg.sh`
   takes `eeg_full.tar` from the current UCI archive (dataset 121) and places it where wbml's loader
   looks, so wbml's own extraction and parser run unchanged.

## Out of the copula's training distribution (flag when reading the numbers)

- Context sizes: Bruinsma's EEG contexts reach ~1,800 rows (7 × 256 minus targets), above the copulas'
  training range P ≤ 1000; Hassan's run from 56 to 1,344 rows.
- Dependence structure: the copulas were trained on synthetic GP draws over features with no
  output-channel structure; the cross-electrode correlation of EEG is out of prior.
- Not trained on EEG: our rows are zero-shot, the published rows were trained on the EEG training
  subjects. TabPFN-3.5's and Kumo's pretraining data are not fully documented; we know of no
  overlap with the UCI EEG Database, but cannot rule it out.

## Findings (2026-11-20; full tables in [eeg_results.md](eeg_results.md))

Nats per target value, higher is better; copies of `results/npbench/eeg/report.md`.

1. **The copula improves on independent sampling from the same marginal on every task and marginal,
   and leaves the marginals unchanged.** Paired gains (± 1.96 SE): Bruinsma interpolation
   +0.034 to +0.041 (4,096 tasks; win rate 0.91–0.98), reconstruction +0.22 to +0.29; Hassan forecasting
   +0.13 to +1.06 (win rate ≥ 0.94); Hassan interpolation +0.19 at N = 8 down to +0.001 to +0.025 at
   N = 192. Copula draws pass the same marginal KS check as independent draws (0.0380 vs 0.0382),
   diag Σ = 1 to 2.4e-7.
2. **The marginal's own autoregressive chain is much stronger** (0.2–1.8 nats per value above the copula
   on the same episodes). Kumo's and TabPFN's chains approach the EEG-trained TNPs on forecasting
   (0.62–0.78 vs TNP-D-AR 1.07 at M = 16). TabICL's chain degrades on long chains with small context
   (forecasting, M = 64, N ≤ 16).
3. **Against the published, EEG-trained models, the zero-shot rows are well behind.** On Bruinsma
   interpolation the copula rows reach −1.62 to −1.74 against ConvCNP −1.02 and ConvCNP (AR) −0.34; on
   Hassan forecasting (M = 16) the best copula row is −0.55 against TNP-ND −0.04. The marginals
   themselves are weaker (indep −1.66 to −1.78 vs ConvCNP −1.02, which is also a marginal-only model).
4. **Joint sampling is cheap.** 8 episodes × 8 joint samples of 112 / 448 target values: copula 1.0–1.4 s
   (incl. the K-fold context PIT), AR 112–1,184 s, i.e. 115–1,025× slower (table in eeg_results.md;
   A100 for TabICL/TabPFN, A40 for Kumo; AR without KV cache, as our TFMs have none).

### Why the copula falls short: a failure case, out of its training prior

`debug/eeg_attainable_copula.py` (Hassan forecasting, all 897 trials, M = 16): a Gaussian copula whose
correlation is estimated on the other half of the trials (cross-fitted, an oracle-ish reference, not a
method) reaches +0.21 to +0.79 against −0.54 to −0.75 for the model's copula and −0.89 to −1.64 for
independence. The copula predicts correlations of 0.05–0.29 between adjacent time points (empirical
0.95) and ≈ 0.0–0.14 between electrodes at the same time (empirical ≈ 0.72), and predicts less as N
grows. The Gaussian-copula family is not the bottleneck; the copula's predicted dependence is, as EEG's
near-collinear channels and 256 Hz smoothness lie outside its synthetic-GP training prior.
`debug/eeg_copula_encoding.py` shows the electrode encoding is not the cause (ordinal or time-only
features for the copula change the score by ≤ 0.06 nats; one-hot was fixed before scoring and kept).

### Comparability caveats

- Our rows are zero-shot; the published rows were trained on the EEG training subjects.
- Hassan et al.: their split has 896 test / 7,802 train trials, ours (Bruinsma's seed-99 split)
  897 / 7,848; normalisation per value vs per time point is ambiguous (see Deviations 2).
- AR rows: 64 (Bruinsma) and 32 × 6 (Hassan) evenly spread episodes, 1 resp. 4 orders.
